Sales of new single-family houses in October 2026 were at a seasonally-adjusted annual rate of 701,000, according to estimates
released jointly today by the U.S. Census Bureau and the Department of Housing and Urban Development. This is 14.2 percent
(±22.6 percent)* above the September 2026 rate of 614,000, and is 7.5 percent (±23.0 percent)* above the October 2025 rate
of 652,000.

For Sale Inventory and Months’ Supply
The seasonally-adjusted estimate of new houses for sale at the end of October 2026 was 470,000. This is 1.5 percent (±1.4
percent) below the September 2026 estimate of 477,000, and is 4.1 percent (±4.1 percent) below the October 2025 estimate of
490,000.

This represents a supply of 8.0 months at the current sales rate. The months' supply is 14.0 percent (±21.7 percent)* below the
September 2026 estimate of 9.3 months, and is 11.1 percent (±20.6 percent)* below the October 2025 estimate of 9.0 months.

Sales Price
The median sales price of new houses sold in October 2026 was $391,300. This is 0.3 percent (±10.9 percent)* below the
September 2026 price of $392,600, and is 3.2 percent (±9.2 percent)* below the October 2025 price of $404,200. The average
sales price of new houses sold in October 2026 was $459,900. This is 2.2 percent (±9.3 percent)* below the September 2026
price of $470,200, and is 12.1 percent (±8.1 percent) below the October 2025 price of $523,400.

EXPLANATORY NOTES
These statistics are estimated from sample surveys. They are subject to sampling variability as well as nonsampling
error including bias and variance from response, nonreporting, and undercoverage. Estimated average relative
standard errors of the preliminary data are shown in the tables. Whenever a statement such as “2.5 percent
(±3.2%) above” appears in the text, this indicates the range (-0.7 to +5.7 percent) in which the actual percent change
is likely to have occurred. All ranges given for percent changes are 90-percent confidence intervals and account only
for sampling variability. If a range does not contain zero, the change is statistically significant. If it does contain zero,
the change is not statistically significant; that is, it is uncertain whether there was an increase or decrease. The same
policies apply to the confidence intervals for percent changes shown in the tables. Changes in seasonally adjusted
statistics often show irregular movement. It takes 4 months to establish a trend for new houses sold. Preliminary
new home sales figures are subject to revision due to the survey methodology and definitions used. The survey is
primarily based on a sample of houses selected from building permits. Since a “sale” is defined as a deposit taken or
sales agreement signed, this can occur prior to a permit being issued. An estimate of these prior sales is included in
the sales figure. On average, the preliminary seasonally adjusted estimate of total sales is revised about 6.7 percent.
Changes in sales price data reflect changes in the distribution of houses by region, size, etc., as well as changes in the
prices of houses with identical characteristics. Explanations of confidence intervals and sampling variability can be
found on our website.

The Census Bureau has reviewed SOC monthly and quarterly tables to ensure appropriate access, use, and disclosure
avoidance protection of the confidential source data (Disclosure Review Board (DRB) approval number: CBDRB-
FY26-0250).
